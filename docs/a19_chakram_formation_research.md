## Geometry and variations

The engine measures footprint-to-footprint Chebyshev separation and requires an empty bridge between
bodies. One empty cell permits full bounce damage; two permit a terminal half-damage hop. Touching
units do not relay directly, but rectangular bodies can leave routes around their edges. A row of
mutually touching neighbours is not sufficient: more distant members can still have a clear bridge.
The clockwise route also depends on the initial shooter bearing and selected primary target.

The candidate family compares rear touching walls, filled blocks and two compact pods separated by
at least four empty lateral cells. The deterministic robust selector includes stock, both lateral
orientations, two own-unit orders and available packing depths. It evaluates all primary targets
from eight hypothetical shooter bearings, both intact and with each single mobile or fragile body
removed. Its frozen objective combines worst and mean weighted collateral exposure, post-removal
exposure, guard adjacency, clustering and carry depth. It uses own units and funded legal cells;
no actual enemy position, quantity, split, artifact, doctrine, augment, seed or outcome enters that
choice. The public drafted Zena identity selects the declared research population.

These are complete setups: unchanged P0/A3/M1/S3 plus utility or no splits; P1/A3/M0/S3 and
P2/A2/M0/S3 without splits. Paid space is charged against actual combat stats. Every plan spends
seven points, respects authoritative Nature-adjusted stack capacity and preserves original supply.
Split children receive the engine's complete independent spellbooks/quivers. Real rectangular
footprints, including 2x1 bodies, are used throughout.

## Full-game explanations

Seventy-two exact rich replays cover 36 references: all eight variants on the original board 1
condition, all six current-versus-robust conditions on both development regressions (11 and 77),
and favorable matched cases. All headless transcripts reproduce their original opening, searched
roots, root inputs, ownership, choices, result and score. Full-event replays match those roots,
choices, score and final unit state; only the event-derived terminal action counter is excluded.
The viewer retains all 15 conditions, 36 branches and 1,817 frames.

- Board 1 / mirror 2 / stream 0 / spread: current Zena's first flight has four victims and 473 HP of reported
  damage. Robust utility splits reduce that first flight to a single 19-HP split Medusa casualty,
  with no collateral, and win. Robust no-splits also prevents collateral throughout this condition
  but loses. Removing bounces alone does not choose the best full-game outcome.
- Board 11 / mirror 2 / current enemy: both setups give Gargantuan 340 HP at its first turn. Current
  Troll starts on its wing, moves forward and intercepts Berserker. The pod arrangement places Troll
  on the opposite wing; Berserker reaches the full-HP Gargantuan and kills it on lap two. Current
  wins with three Gargantuan ranged actions; robust loses after one. This is an observed protection
  failure, not a reason to select a hidden enemy deployment before battle.
- Board 77 / mirror 3 / reflected enemy: current Cyclops fires on Magic Dragon for 166 reported HP;
  the pod Cyclops moves on its first turn. Both raw native incumbents proposed ranged attacks, so
  the latter is a search override rather than absence of every legal shot. The pod branch later
  loses. Whether forcing a particular shot improves expected continuation needs its own experiment;
  this one trajectory cannot prove a ranking error.

The next formation representation should preserve protection and productive attack opportunities
while preventing bridge chains. Which units occupy each pod matters as much as the empty space
between them. These observations do not retune or promote the frozen policy from its test outcomes.

primary and other current bodies fixed, all eight combinations of those moves are checked. Returning
all three coordinates blocks every secondary hit; moving **only Medusa** from its starting cell
already creates a four-victim full-damage chain. A moved unit can open a former bridge blocker
and become a relay elsewhere, so deleting one body is not a proxy for moving it. These static
counterfactuals are geometric explanations, not played alternative full games.
