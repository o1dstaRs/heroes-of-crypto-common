## Fixed candidate

The helper `src/simulation/peasant_followthrough_research.ts` compares only an acting Peasant with
active Bitter Experience. After the ordinary material/intent decision, but **before the unchanged
final neural correction**, it may prefer another already-retained same-target attack square.

Both choices must have complete H64/complementary-R2 results, tie at the primary maximum, and kill
the same target in both root samples. Each paired sample must leave identical immediate actor HP,
bodies, armor, steps and Bitter stacks, identical supported cheap-response price projections, and
identical full state of every other surviving unit. Unsafe terrain moves and composite actions are
excluded. The alternate square must be at least one Euclidean cell nearer the closest remaining
visible shooter or usable offensive-spell caster in **both** samples, without becoming adjacent to an
additional enemy. Lowest average distance wins, then original catalog order.

This is a geometric tie-break, not proof of safe next-turn reach or a calibrated value estimate.
It adds no candidates or rollouts and changes no search score, model weights, neural margin or
continuation policy. Sampled outcome equivalence is not a guarantee of equivalence over all chance.
Unknown, incomplete or inconsistent evidence retains the original decision. No serving file imports
the helper. The old backline movement and post-move helpers remain immutable; only their pure public
output classifier and response-price inspector are reused by the private observer.

In all six comparisons, both Peasants attacked on their next own turn, in the same lap. Five attacked
the same next target. All four Peasants that eventually died still died in the same lap; the other two
survived in both arms. Later HP changed in both directions, so closer placement was not uniformly safer.
The recorded steps at the first changed roots were already 7–13. These traces show why geometric
approach alone need not create an earlier attack; they do not isolate every later response or random
event as a causal explanation of the final result.

The actor-equivalence guard matches HP, bodies, armor, steps and Bitter stacks plus response price.
It does not compare every actor effect or resistance. The other surviving units are fully matched in
the paired root samples. The geometric distance also ignores legal path length, target importance and
future exposure. Those limitations remain part of the closed candidate; no threshold or guard was
changed after results began.
