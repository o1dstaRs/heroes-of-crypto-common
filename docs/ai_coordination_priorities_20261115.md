## Combat: distinguish a projection gap from a useful complete policy

The ordinary melee catalog computes damage from a rough minimum/maximum midpoint and labels a kill
when that estimate reaches remaining HP. Unlike the richer shared primary-hit projection, this estimate
does not account for the complete supported damage distribution, miss probability, elemental affinity,
position/charge effects or per-outcome overkill. Real-engine rollouts still resolve those mechanics.
The issue is which options survive the cap and how their metadata reaches later arbitration, not a
claim that the combat resolver ignores them.

A new isolated prototype, `stationary-melee-value-v1`, replaces only supported stationary, unanswered
primary-hit metadata before the unchanged cap/search. Moving attacks, possible retaliation, secondary
damage, redirection and resurrection keep the incumbent estimate. It consumes no RNG, adds no rollout,
changes no model weights and is not imported by a serving policy. Its sampled miss probability respects
the engine's integer roll, and a secure-kill flag requires every possible landed roll to kill with no miss.

The ten regression tests include an 800-outcome comparison with actual resolved attacks. With a 21–28
damage band, 25 remaining HP and 20.5% configured miss chance, legacy metadata says 25 damage/a kill;
the engine expectation is lower and a kill is not certain. The new prototype matches the resolved mean.
Tests also cover elemental damage, the native ranged melee penalty, shield consumption, state/RNG
isolation and unsupported-sequence fallbacks. This is projection correctness, not win-rate evidence.
