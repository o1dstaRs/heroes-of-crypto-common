# Offensive Chaos Movement research

The user proposed drafting mobile attackers and activating Chaos Movement to catch opposing shooters or
damage casters, with funded Movement, Armor and forward Placement. This is a separate offensive study;
the earlier failed studies protecting shooters do not answer this question.

**Research only.** The adopted ranked draft, setup, formation and v0.8/A19 combat defaults remain unchanged.
The helper in `src/simulation/chaos_rush_research.ts` is not a serving policy. The complete development
comparison closed without a finalist. No candidate was changed after registration, no new seed bank was
allocated, and no fresh qualification or confirmation ran.

## Completed decision

All 8,079 distinct battles completed: 16,848 directly simulated logical rows, plus the prospectively fixed
Chaos-only compositions, for 52 evaluated policies. Independent implementations reproduced all paired
scores, selection decisions and simultaneous intervals. No policy passed the development gate, and none
had a positive simultaneous lower confidence bound against current behavior.

The strongest observed policy was `chaos-gated/p1-m2-armor`: public Chaos Movement dispatch, the mobile/Chaos
draft, Movement 2, Placement 1, Armor-first remaining spending and forward formation when the resulting
own draft activates the synergy. It scored 212 wins, two draws and 110 losses on 324 primary seat/order
games, versus 202 wins, two draws and 120 losses for current behavior: **65.740741% versus 62.654321%**.
Its **+3.086420 pp** gain has a simultaneous 95% interval of **−2.875348 to +9.048188 pp**. Against its
matched free formation, the gain was **+1.851852 pp**, interval **−3.122945 to +6.826649 pp**.

This is a promising development observation, not a qualified improvement. It failed the fixed doctrine
gate: five-point gain +4.166667 pp, six-point −2.777778 pp, seven-point +7.870370 pp. Public Movement
occurred in 11, 10 and 10 primary families respectively. The negative six-point estimate does not establish
an intrinsic doctrine weakness, and selecting only the favorable five-/seven-point slices would be a new,
outcome-selected candidate. The unchanged recipe does not advance. All intervals below adjust for all
75 planned comparisons; the complete 52-policy table is retained in the standalone report.

The delivery mechanism works without establishing a net win. The full ungated Placement 2 rush reached
melee contact with a backliner in 98.46% of primary cases versus 71.91% for current behavior. Conditional
mean contact lap fell from 2.87 to 1.27 and enemy backline actions before contact or fight end fell from
8.46 to 1.72. Its final score nevertheless fell 6.94 pp. Faster arrival must repay the changed draft,
removed damage/protection and altered formation; reaching a caster does not remove its spell option.

The evidence supports preserving a selective one-point-placement lead for separately designed research,
while rejecting a blanket fast-unit drafting override. It does not support enabling this recipe, buying
Placement 2 by default, or creating a five-/seven-point exception from this same bank. Any distinct
continuation needs a new candidate and registration with its own evaluation boundaries. Do not tune this
closed candidate on its completed outcomes or relabel the reused families as fresh confirmation.

## What the candidates can see and change

- Draft restrictions use only the phase's legally revealed enemy identities. The existing native scorer
  selects within the mobile or Chaos-completing legal pool and retains its artifact, ban and map context.
  Hidden final rosters are never passed backward into the draft.
- Setup uses the completed public enemy roster, its own roster, the public synergy variant and its real
  five-, six- or seven-point budget. Hidden enemy upgrades and deployment are not inputs.
- A threat is a native shooter or a creature with a native direct-damage ranged spell. The catalog's
  `mage` flag alone is insufficient: Battle Mage, Wandering Mage and Magic Dragon are melee-magic units.
  Utility-only spellbooks are excluded from this threat classifier.
- Mobile melee excludes native shooters and direct-damage spellcasters, and admits flight or at least five
  printed steps. Flight admits Griffin without discarding Sky Runner's additional movement.
- Chaos completion counts distinct creature types at the 2/4/6 thresholds and only values the actual public
  Movement variant. Splitting a creature does not add another faction type.
- Funded recipes cross Movement 0/1/2, Placement spend 0/1/2, Armor-first or Might-first remaining points,
  and native or forward deployment. The forward rule moves melee bodies toward the opposing side while
  preserving legal rectangular footprints and staying within two lateral cells of native positions.
  It runs after native final placement and before artifact barrels. Actual native splits and complete
  caster spellbooks remain part of every battle.
- Combat decisions remain the current A19 policy. This study does not change its tactical candidate
  catalog, target scoring, search budget or micro policy.

Every paid plan has a matched free plan with the same draft, Movement level, remaining-point priority and
formation algorithm. Freed Placement points buy real combat stats. For example, at five points,
Placement 2 plus Movement 2 leaves Armor 1; free placement plus Movement 2 can retain Armor 3. Armor also
adds magic resistance, so its opportunity cost matters against casters as well as physical shooters.

## Analysis and decision gate

The primary target is the first 81 families: current, shooter and caster opponents. The remaining 27
pressure families test safety. Draws score one half. Each independent family contributes the average of
its four seat/order permutations. Twenty thousand paired whole-family bootstrap resamples provide
simultaneous 95% intervals across 51 comparisons with current behavior and 24 paid-versus-free comparisons.

At most one unchanged recipe can advance from development: at least one percentage point against current
and, for paid plans, against matched free; nonnegative means for all three own budgets and against the
current opponent; and at least minus two percentage points against pressure. Ties prefer the higher target
score, then lower Placement spending, then the fixed lexical identifier. A favorable subgroup cannot
replace this gate. Advancement would still require separately registered fresh qualification and independent
confirmation under the [Placement promotion requirements](placement_research_promotion.md).

First melee contact, enemy backline actions and deployment changes are diagnostics. A physical shooter may
already be denied a shot by engagement or Range Null Field before the first hit. A separate, prospectively
fixed 36-fight replay diagnostic records those conditions and caster behavior at actual decision entry;
each replay must reproduce its complete turn/event hash. These replays add no independent strength samples.

All 36 fixed diagnostic replays reproduced their complete original turn/event hashes. The following counts
come from the first five laps of the nine primary cases per policy; three additional pressure cases per
policy are preserved separately. Counts depend on roster changes, survival, waits and extra turns. They

The rush reaches backliners and increases shooting denial in these replays. Actual shooting turns fall
much less than the denial share suggests, because the fights also contain different rosters and numbers
of surviving turns. Contact does not silence damage casters: engaged casters successfully cast Lightning
Strike, Fireball and Meteor Shower. A physical shooter can also regain eligibility by leaving engagement
or the disabling aura; entry denial is not a permanent status.

The unscored full census also establishes the strategy's coverage. On the 324 primary seat/order cases,
the current draft activates Chaos Movement in 52 cases, all level 1. Chaos-focused drafting activates it
in 100 cases: 86 level 1 and 14 level 2. No level 3 occurs. The current opponent averages 3.24 native shooter
types and 0.67 damage-caster types; the shooter-focused opponent averages 3.78 and 0.56, and the
caster-focused opponent 2.46 and 1.52. These are legal mixed armies, not forced six-caster rosters.

The adopted setup buys no Movement on these primary cases. Its common spending is Armor plus Sniper;
the proposed rush therefore exchanges actual ranged damage and sometimes Armor for mobility. Relative to
the matched free Movement 2 plan, five-point Placement 2 / Movement 2 reduces Armor from level 3's +21
magic armor to level 1's +6, before independent resistance abilities. This cost applies even when contact
happens sooner.

## Admission and reproducibility

The unscored census checked all native setups, budgets, footprints, legal draft choices and mirrored
inventories. A pre-registration classifier correction is preserved separately: offensive spellcasters
must use `rangedSpellDamage`, rather than the printed `mage` flag. No scored candidate was tuned by that
correction.

The full-battle preflight completed 193 distinct battles representing 468 logical rows. Unmodified baseline
traces reproduced the previous reference. Six cold replays on each of the local Mac, Puffalo and 9800X3D
matched complete inputs, formations, draft receipts, turn/events, outcomes, diagnostics and operation counts;
only elapsed time is excluded. Actual movement checks verified that Chaos Movement also increases non-Chaos
teammates' steps.

Source and package identity are asserted, including the exact common RNG and fight-state singleton used by
the copied server. Runtime unit identities are seeded. Source dependencies and policy inputs remain frozen
across hosts. An orchestration-only reshard preserved 37 complete Puffalo groups and assigned only unstarted
groups to additional capacity; host outputs remain separate and no policy, seed or statistical rule changed.

The local research capsule is the client repository's ignored `game/core/tmp/chaos-rush-20261115` directory.
Registrations, sources, raw games, receipts and the standalone report stay outside Git. A concise completed
decision belongs here; generated games and reports must not be committed as ordinary source changes.

The final provenance verifier recomputed every recorded input, formation and draft-receipt hash, matched
all 16,848 rows to the unscored census and confirmed the exact 432-group host partition. All 5,357 frozen
source/dependency files remained intact. Both remote downloads matched their source file hashes. All
development games had zero rejected actions, neural errors or missing decision starts. The common build,
root lint/type checks and all 13,973 repository tests passed. The isolation regression keeps this helper
out of the public entry point, serving setup and battle/search drivers.

The study tests fixed draft restrictions and funded formations under deterministic A19 combat from native
ranked openings. It does not retrain artifacts or combat tactics, simulate human opponents, cover level-3
Chaos Movement, or classify every spell gained later through artifacts or stolen abilities. Its numerical
results are tied to the frozen source, not an overall ranked matchmaking win rate.
